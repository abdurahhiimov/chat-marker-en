Chat Marker · version 1.1

A marker for everything you read in the browser

You highlight a thought right on the page — and it stays yours for good: in a topic, in the shared feed, in the spreadsheet, in a Google Drive document and in Claude's memory. No copy-paste, no "saved tabs", no "Read later" folder.

The problem this grew out of

1A long back-and-forth with an AI

A good formulation turned up in the middle of the chat. A week later you can't find it: chat search looks for words, not meaning, and doesn't remember what you liked.

2Copy-paste into notes

You copied it and lost the source. A month later it's unclear where the quote came from and what you meant when you saved it.

3Bookmarks don't work

You save the whole page when what you needed was one paragraph. Two hundred bookmarks isn't a library, it's a dump.

What it looks like in use

Highlight it, pick a topic

A little panel pops up next to the selection. One click and the piece is painted in the topic's color. No topic for it yet — you create one right there, without leaving the text.

The highlight lives on top of the page, not inside it: the chat can keep drawing out its answer, the page can repaint — the highlight stays where it was.

Everything you collected in one place

The panel shows highlights from every site at once: chats, articles, documentation. Search across text and notes, filters by topic, and the "to the spot" link takes you back to exactly the paragraph you took it from.

The database is shared across all sites — what you marked in Claude shows up on a Wikipedia page and the other way round.

What you get out of it

A folder in Google Drive

Topics, sources, an index, an Excel spreadsheet and a dashboard. It builds itself and syncs to every device.

A live document per topic

Mark a topic and you get a .docx that updates itself on every export. You can hand the link to a colleague, the contents are always fresh.

Claude sees your highlights

"What have I collected on unit economics?" — and it answers from your quotes, not from the internet. Over MCP, no copy-paste.

A link to the exact spot

Every highlight remembers the page and the paragraph. Click and the browser opens the source and scrolls where it needs to on its own.

What you need for this to work

WhatWhat for
Chrome + TampermonkeyThe marker itself. The extension is free, the script gets pasted in once.
Google Drive for desktopThe library folder sits on disk locally and syncs to the cloud.
Claude DesktopSo you can ask questions about your own highlights and ask for a document to be built.
Python (installs itself)The library builder and the MCP server.

Installation is one script and two steps by hand. After that the tool runs without maintenance.

What it doesn't do — honestly

  • Doesn't work in Safari without some dancing: the extensions there are different, Chrome is what's supported out of the box.
  • Doesn't highlight images and PDFs — only text on a web page.
  • Doesn't sync highlights between two computers in real time: the shared memory is the Drive folder.
  • Doesn't draw conclusions for you. The analysis appears when you ask Claude to work through a topic.
